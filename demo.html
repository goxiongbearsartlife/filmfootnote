Night Movies / Issue 07

Vampires, Rebellion,
and the Outsiders

A short note on films and television that turn adolescence into a nocturnal landscape: neon, danger, desire, and the feeling that adulthood is arriving too fast.

Few films of the 1980s capture the collision of adolescence and the supernatural quite like The Lost Boys. Joel Schumacher's film turns Santa Cruz into a neon-lit dream of motorcycles, comic books, boardwalks, and beautiful strangers who may not be what they seem.

Decades earlier, Alfred Hitchcock's Psycho transformed an ordinary roadside motel into one of cinema's most enduring spaces of anxiety. Its influence survives not only in horror, but in the way films teach us to distrust familiar architecture.

Television inherited many of those ideas. Twin Peaks folds melodrama, murder mystery, and dream logic into a town where every room seems to conceal another version of reality.

The more recent Severance turns the office into a psychological labyrinth, where architecture becomes both corporate system and fractured memory.

And then there is On the Silver Globe, a work that feels less like conventional science fiction than an archaeological fragment from a civilization that never existed.
